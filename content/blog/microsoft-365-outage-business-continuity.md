## What Happened During the November 12 Microsoft Outage?

Microsoft confirmed a widespread service disruption affecting multiple Microsoft 365 services on November 12, 2026.

The outage primarily affected Exchange Online, leaving some users unable to access their mailboxes through Outlook, web browsers, and mobile devices. Email delivery was also delayed.

Other Microsoft 365 services experienced varying levels of disruption, including features within Microsoft Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft Copilot, and several administrative and security tools.

Microsoft identified problems involving overloaded and unresponsive infrastructure and began restoring affected services. As of November 13, recovery was progressing, although some users continued experiencing intermittent connectivity issues and delayed email delivery.

Microsoft stated that it had no evidence of malicious activity associated with the incident. A final root cause had not yet been established.

The incident demonstrates an important reality: **even the world's largest technology companies can experience service disruptions.**

Businesses need to prepare for that possibility.

## The Hidden Cost of IT Downtime

When a critical technology service stops working, the consequences can spread throughout an organization.

Imagine a business that relies on Microsoft Outlook to receive customer requests, schedule appointments, coordinate employees, and communicate with vendors.

When email suddenly becomes unavailable, work doesn't necessarily stop entirely, but employees may struggle to determine what needs attention or how to communicate with customers.

For some organizations, an hour of disruption is manageable. For others, particularly those handling time-sensitive operations, even a brief interruption can have significant consequences.

Downtime can lead to lost productivity, delayed customer responses, missed opportunities, interrupted workflows, and additional costs.

The impact also extends beyond the immediate outage. Once service is restored, employees may need to process delayed messages, catch up on unfinished work, and determine whether any communications were missed.

This is why business continuity planning is so important.

## What Is a Business Continuity Plan?

A **business continuity plan (BCP)** is a documented strategy that outlines how an organization will maintain essential operations during unexpected disruptions.

These disruptions might include cloud service outages, internet failures, power interruptions, cyberattacks, natural disasters, or hardware failures.

Business continuity planning goes beyond traditional backups and disaster recovery.

While disaster recovery focuses primarily on restoring systems and data, business continuity focuses on keeping the organization operational while those systems are unavailable.

For example, if Microsoft 365 becomes inaccessible, a business continuity plan should answer questions such as:

- How will employees communicate with one another?
- How will customers contact the organization?
- Which business processes can continue without email?
- Who is responsible for communicating updates?
- What alternative systems or procedures are available?
- How will normal operations resume once service is restored?

Having these answers documented before an outage allows businesses to respond more quickly and with less confusion.

## Six Ways to Prepare Your Business for the Next Outage

### 1. Identify Your Most Critical Technology

Not every application has the same importance to your organization.

Start by identifying the systems your employees depend on most. These may include email, phone systems, accounting applications, customer relationship management software, file storage, or industry-specific platforms.

Determine how long each system can realistically remain unavailable before it causes serious operational problems.

This process helps prioritize which systems need the strongest contingency measures.

### 2. Establish Alternative Communication Methods

If email becomes unavailable, employees and customers still need a way to communicate.

Businesses should establish approved alternative methods, such as phone calls, text messaging, or a separate communication platform that does not depend on the affected service.

Make sure critical contact information is available outside Microsoft 365.

The important detail is independence. An alternative communication system isn't particularly useful if it relies on the same unavailable Microsoft services to authenticate users.

### 3. Implement Reliable Backups and Disaster Recovery

Cloud services provide significant advantages, but they should not eliminate the need for a thoughtful data protection strategy.

Organizations should evaluate independent backups of critical Microsoft 365 data, including Exchange Online, OneDrive, and SharePoint.

These backups can help recover information following accidental deletion, malicious activity, or data corruption.

However, backups and service availability are different things. Having a backup of your email does not automatically mean you can continue sending and receiving messages during a Microsoft outage.

A complete strategy should address both data recovery and operational continuity.

### 4. Document Alternative Workflows

Sometimes the simplest contingency measures can be the most effective.

If an online scheduling system becomes unavailable, can appointments be managed through another approved process?

If an accounting platform is inaccessible, can essential transactions be documented temporarily and entered once service returns?

Employees should understand which tasks can continue, what information must be recorded, and how that information will be reconciled afterward.

Documenting these procedures reduces confusion and helps maintain productivity.

### 5. Define Recovery Priorities

Every organization should understand two important concepts:

**Recovery Time Objective (RTO):** How quickly a critical system or business process needs to be restored.

**Recovery Point Objective (RPO):** How much data loss, measured in time, the organization can reasonably tolerate.

These objectives help businesses make informed decisions about backup solutions, system redundancy, and recovery investments.

A small office may be able to tolerate a longer interruption than a healthcare provider, manufacturer, or organization responsible for essential public services.

Your recovery strategy should reflect your actual business requirements.

### 6. Test Your Business Continuity Plan

A plan that has never been tested is simply a collection of assumptions.

Businesses should periodically simulate realistic disruptions to verify that employees understand their responsibilities and that alternative procedures actually work.

Testing can reveal overlooked dependencies, outdated contact information, or systems that don't function as independently as expected.

The goal isn't to create an overly complicated process. It's to make sure your organization knows what to do when technology becomes unavailable.

## What Should You Do During a Microsoft 365 Outage?

When employees begin reporting problems with Outlook, Teams, or other Microsoft applications, the first step is identifying whether the issue is local to your organization or part of a broader service disruption.

Microsoft publishes incident updates through the Service Health dashboard in the Microsoft 365 admin center.

If Microsoft confirms a service-wide incident, repeatedly reinstalling applications, resetting passwords, or making unnecessary configuration changes generally won't resolve the underlying problem.

Instead, organizations should focus on activating their business continuity procedures, keeping employees informed, and identifying which operations can continue using approved alternatives.

Security should remain a priority. An outage is not a reason to bypass multifactor authentication, weaken access controls, or introduce untested systems that could create additional problems.

Once service is restored, organizations should verify that important systems are functioning correctly and review any delayed communications or interrupted workflows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an IT provider prevent Microsoft 365 outages?

No managed IT provider can guarantee that Microsoft or another cloud service provider will never experience an outage. However, an IT provider can help minimize the impact through monitoring, contingency planning, alternate communication methods, and properly designed recovery procedures.

### Does Microsoft 365 automatically back up all my business data?

Microsoft provides service-level resilience and data protection capabilities, but these may not meet every organization's recovery and retention requirements. Businesses should evaluate their specific needs and consider independent backups for important email, files, and other Microsoft 365 information.

### Can a business continue operating when Microsoft 365 is down?

Often, yes. The extent depends on how heavily the organization relies on affected services and whether alternative communication methods, accessible business records, and documented workflows are already in place.

### How often should a business continuity plan be tested?

Organizations should test their plans periodically and whenever significant changes are made to critical systems or business processes. The appropriate frequency depends on the organization's risks, operational requirements, and compliance obligations.
