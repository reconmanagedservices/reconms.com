## 2. Look for Proactive IT Support, Not Just Reactive Repairs

One of the most important distinctions between IT providers is how they approach problems.

Some providers operate primarily on a break-fix model. When something stops working, you call for help, and they repair it.

While there's nothing inherently wrong with that approach, it doesn't necessarily encourage preventing problems before they occur.

A proactive managed IT provider takes a different approach.

Instead of waiting for an employee to report that a server is offline, a computer is experiencing problems, or a backup has failed, proactive IT management uses monitoring, maintenance, and planning to identify potential issues earlier.

This can include monitoring hardware health, maintaining software updates, reviewing backup activity, identifying security vulnerabilities, and monitoring network performance.

The objective is simple: **reduce avoidable interruptions and keep employees productive.**

Of course, no provider can eliminate every technology problem. But a proactive approach can make a meaningful difference in how frequently issues arise and how effectively they're handled.

## 3. Make Sure Cybersecurity Is Part of the Conversation

Cybersecurity is no longer something businesses can afford to treat as an optional service.

Whether your organization has five employees or five hundred, protecting business information, employee accounts, and critical systems should be a priority.

Modern cyberattacks frequently target ordinary business activities, such as email communication, password management, and remote access.

A convincing phishing email or compromised employee account can create significant problems for an organization.

When evaluating an IT company, ask how they approach cybersecurity.

Do they help protect computers and servers? Do they support multifactor authentication? Are they monitoring for security threats? Do they help train employees to recognize suspicious activity?

Just as importantly, do they have a plan for responding if something goes wrong?

A good IT provider should be able to explain your organization's cybersecurity risks in plain language and recommend practical improvements.

## 7. Ask About Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

For businesses in Southeast Texas, disaster recovery planning is especially important.

Hurricanes, flooding, severe storms, power failures, internet outages, and cyberattacks can interrupt business operations with little warning.

A reliable IT provider should help you prepare for these scenarios.

Having backups is important, but simply creating backups isn't the same as having a business continuity plan.

What happens if your office loses internet access for two days? Can employees work remotely if your building is inaccessible? How quickly could critical files and systems be restored after a major incident?

These are questions worth discussing before an emergency.

A thoughtful IT partner should help identify critical systems, establish recovery priorities, evaluate backup options, and develop practical contingency procedures.
